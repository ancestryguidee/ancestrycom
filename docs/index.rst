With billions of historical records, powerful search tools, and an intuitive family tree builder, Ancestry.com makes it possible for anyone to uncover their roots and preserve their family legacy. Whether you are a beginner just starting your genealogy journey or an experienced researcher, Ancestry.com provides the resources you need to trace your ancestors, connect with relatives, and understand your family's unique story. This comprehensive guide explores every aspect of how to use Ancestry.com, from creating your account and building your family tree to searching records, understanding DNA results, and connecting with the Ancestry community.

Understanding Ancestry.com
==========================

Ancestry.com is a comprehensive genealogy platform that provides access to the world's largest online collection of family history records. With over 60 billion searchable historical records and photographs, the site enables users to discover details about their ancestors' lives, including names, addresses, occupations, military service, and emigration journeys. The platform serves millions of members worldwide and offers tools designed for both beginners and experienced researchers.

Ancestry.com offers multiple pathways to discovering your family story. You can start by building a family tree using the intuitive tree builder tool, search billions of records to find information about your ancestors, or uncover your ethnicity and genetic connections through AncestryDNA. The site also provides professional services through AncestryProGenealogists for those who need expert assistance with their research.

One of the key features of Ancestry.com is its accessibility across devices. You can access your research from any web browser on a computer, as well as through the Ancestry mobile app for iOS and Android devices. This cross-platform availability ensures that you can continue your family history research wherever you are.

Creating Your Ancestry.com Account
==================================

To begin using Ancestry.com, you need to create an account. The registration process is straightforward and requires basic personal information such as your name and email address.

When you register on Ancestry.com, you provide personal information that is used to manage your account. This includes your name, email address, and, if you subscribe to a paid membership, your payment information. Other users cannot see this information except for your name if you choose to share it in your profile settings. Your account also includes a public profile that helps you connect with other users, and you can control what information is displayed in your profile.

Ancestry.com offers several membership options to suit different research needs. The U.S. Discovery Membership provides access to all U.S. records, while the World Explorer Membership includes access to both U.S. and international records. The All Access Membership provides comprehensive access to all 60 billion records from around the world, including everything on Fold3 and Newspapers.com.

Ancestry.com offers a free trial for new users, allowing you to explore the platform and its features before committing to a paid subscription. The free trial period lets you search records, build your family tree, and experience the power of the platform. After the trial, you can choose the membership tier that best fits your research needs and budget.

Building Your Family Tree on Ancestry.com
=========================================

The family tree builder is one of the core features of Ancestry.com. This intuitive tool helps you preserve your family history and organize your discoveries.

To build your family tree, start by entering the names and details you already know about yourself, your parents, and your grandparents. You can add dates of birth, locations, and any other information you have. Ancestry.com uses this information to search for matching records and suggest potential connections.

As you add people to your family tree, Ancestry.com provides hints to help you find more information about your ancestors. When a leaf icon appears, it means that the system has found a record or photo that may match someone in your tree. These Ancestry Hints do the searching for you, recommending records, photos, and other family trees for you to review. Hints can also suggest potential new ancestors to add to your tree.

You own the privacy settings on your family tree. You can choose to make your tree public or private, and you control who can view and edit it. Living people in your tree are always hidden by default to protect their privacy. You can also share your tree with friends and family so they can view it, contribute, or help you edit details.

Searching Historical Records on Ancestry.com
============================================

Ancestry.com provides access to an unparalleled collection of historical records from around the world. These records include census data, birth, marriage, and death records, military records, immigration records, wills, ships' logs, and more.

The search functionality on Ancestry.com is designed to help you find specific information about your ancestors. You can search by name, location, date, and other criteria. The platform also offers advanced search options that allow you to filter by record type, collection, and other parameters. You can even search by year, decade, or era, making it easier to find what you are looking for even if you do not know an exact day or year to start with.

When you view a record on Ancestry.com, the redesigned record page makes it easier to evaluate documents quickly and confidently. The page features a hover-to-zoom capability on the top thumbnail, allowing you to scan the record immediately without opening the full image. An "In your tree" section shows a profile snapshot of the person you are evaluating, including their family members and timeline, so you can compare the record's details against the information you already have. Clear decision buttons prompt you to indicate whether the record matches the person in your tree, and the prompt follows you as you scroll down the page.

Ancestry.com also includes suggested records to help you continue your research without losing momentum. These recommendations appear as you view records, helping you quickly pick and view the most relevant records for your research. The site also shows member research, allowing you to see if other members are researching the same people or records as you.

AncestryDNA on Ancestry.com
===========================

AncestryDNA is an integrated feature of Ancestry.com that provides insights into your ethnicity, genetic relatives, and personal traits. When you purchase an AncestryDNA kit and register it on the site, you can access your DNA results through your account.

Your AncestryDNA results provide detailed information about your ethnic origins, showing the regions around the world where your DNA is connected. Ancestry.com also offers DNA Journeys that reveal where your relatives might have lived or moved in the past 300 years. These features provide historical context for your genetic heritage and connect you to specific geographical areas.

The AncestryDNA section of Ancestry.com also includes a Matches tab that displays other AncestryDNA members who share DNA with you. These are your genetic relatives, and the list is based solely on your DNA, not on family trees. You can view your matches, explore their public trees, and contact them to collaborate on your research. The site also offers features like Matches by Parent and Ethnicity Inheritance, which help you see which ethnicity regions you inherited from each parent without them needing to take a DNA test.

Traits are another feature available through AncestryDNA on Ancestry.com. These show personal characteristics influenced by your DNA, the environment, or both, including physical traits like eye color and behavioral traits such as risk-taking or athletic ability.

Connecting with the Ancestry Community
======================================

Ancestry.com provides numerous ways to connect and collaborate with other members who share your research interests. These community features help you discover new information and connect with potential relatives.

Member Search allows you to find other Ancestry members by name, username, or research interests such as surnames, locations, and years. This is a great way to connect with people who may be researching the same family lines. From the list of results, you can view a member's public profile and send them a message if you would like to collaborate.

Member Connect helps you discover and connect with other Ancestry members who are researching the same people in their trees. You can connect to receive updates when new information is added and compare what is in your tree with theirs. This feature is accessible from any person's profile in your tree.

The Ancestry Message Boards are public forums with more than 25 million posts across 198,000 boards. These boards are a great place to connect with others who share your research interests, ask questions, and exchange information. You can search by location, event, keyword, or browse boards by surname to read existing threads or start new ones.

Private messaging is another way to connect on Ancestry.com. The site's messaging service allows users to contact each other anonymously through the Ancestry website without sharing personal contact information. When another user sends you a message, you will receive an alert in your Ancestry inbox and an email. You can respond through the site without revealing your email address or other contact details.

Managing Your Ancestry.com Account
==================================

Proper account management ensures that you can access your research and control your privacy settings on Ancestry.com. Understanding the available settings helps you customize your experience and protect your personal information.

Your public profile on Ancestry.com displays your username or full name, when you became an Ancestry member, and when you last logged in. You can also add additional personal information such as gender, age, occupation, a photo, location, and research interests. You control what information appears on your profile and can edit it at any time.

Ancestry.com allows you to sign in using your Google or Apple account credentials as an additional option. If you do not want to remember another username and password, you can use your Google or Apple login to access Ancestry. You can disconnect your Ancestry account from these services at any time through the site preferences.

If you need to transfer your research between accounts, Ancestry.com provides methods for moving family tree information. You can download your tree in the form of a GEDCOM file from one account and upload it to another account. You can also copy individual people from one tree to another using the "Save to Tree" tool. It is not currently possible to transfer ownership of a family tree between accounts, but you can share trees and copy information between accounts.
